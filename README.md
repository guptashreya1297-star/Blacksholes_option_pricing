# Black-Scholes Option Pricing Model

## Overview
This project implements the Black-Scholes model in Python to calculate the theoretical prices of European call and put options. It explores the mathematical principles of option pricing and the relationship between call and put options through put-call parity.

## Objectives
- Understand the mathematical foundation of the Black-Scholes option pricing model.
- Implement the Black-Scholes formulas to calculate European call and put option prices.
- Analyze put-call parity and its role in evaluating option pricing consistency.

## Model Inputs
- **S:** Current stock price
- **K:** Strike price of the option
- **T:** Time to maturity (in years)
- **r:** Risk-free interest rate
- **σ:** Annualized volatility of the underlying asset

## Key Concepts
- **Black-Scholes Model:** Estimates the theoretical prices of European call and put options based on stock price, strike price, time to maturity, risk-free interest rate, and volatility.
- **Put-Call Parity:** Explains the pricing relationship between European call and put options with the same strike price and expiration date.

## Technologies Used

`Black_Scholes_Final_Put_Call_Parity_.ipynb`

## Limitations
The Black-Scholes model is based on simplifying assumptions, including constant volatility and a constant risk-free interest rate. Actual market prices may differ due to dividends, transaction costs, liquidity constraints, and changing market conditions.
